What this does and does not measure. SATAT has no field-verified ground truth: nobody has visited these coordinates to confirm what was burning. What can be measured is whether thermal physics and timing alone predict something they were never shown: whether a detection sits on a site two or more independent registries agree exists. High scores mean the physics and the registries agree, not that a cause is confirmed.

importance

Distance and corroboration are deliberately absent. Including them would let the model restate its own labels, which is what made an earlier version's accuracy meaningless.

{{ icon('info') }}
Two limits worth stating. FIRMS publishes two I-band channels; NOAA EOG's Nightfire, which catalogues flares, uses short-wave M-bands that constrain high temperatures far better. And VIIRS I4 saturates at 367 K, above which the retrieval returns a floor. The hottest sources are the ones SATAT pins down least, so a snapshot can legitimately contain almost no flare_signature detections.
